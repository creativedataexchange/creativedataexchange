About Creative Data Exchange

New technology has been disrupting creative practice since before the invention of the paintbrush. But perhaps it has been some time since it had the potential to fundamentally change the tools, materials and social relations of creative practice globally.

And as with most big changes, the Data & AI hype emerged around serious ethical and safety concerns, as well as mysticism, driving curiosity but also provoking anger and hesitation to engage.

"Creative Data Exchange" is the antidote to that. It provides a space for open conversations about creative work with data, to share your questions and ambitions and to find routes to meaningful change.

Creative Data Exchange is presented by the Contemporary Narratives Lab in partnership with the Alan Turing Institute and Goldsmiths University of London.

What are we inviting you for?

Up to 2 hours of a conversation about creative practice, AI and data.

The driving questions are:

  • "how can data and AI help develop (your) creative practice?" and
  • "how can creative practice help develop data & AI technologies?"

And you can further shape the agenda with your questions by signing up to attend the free call.

What happens after the conversation?

We are forming a community of interested artists, creatives, and researchers to keep the conversation going. You will be invited to join the Alan Turing Institute's AI&Arts group, which is a space for anyone interested in the intersection of AI and art.

Your contributions to the online calls will feed a free programme of in-person, hands-on, workshops to be advertised to the community first.

Who is going to be there?

We are inviting experienced creative practitioners with little to no experience using data who are curious to learn more.

The people facilitating the meeting will be:

André Piza, who is leading this project as a Software Sustainability Institute Fellow 2026, is a community developer at heart, with a background as a creative practitioner. He has written about cultural exchange practice in The Art of Cultural Exchange (2019) and is an Organiser of the Art of Cultural Exchange. More recently, he has been supporting institutional change towards more ethical and sustainable practices in creative tech.

Kaspar Beelen is a digital historian who explores the impact of datafication and machine learning on humanities research. After obtaining his interdisciplinary PhD in historical network analysis, he has been exploring how to make data and AI literacy more accessible to practitioners in the arts, culture and humanities. He works as a Researcher at the Alan Turing Institute.

Rosie Wood is a Research Software Engineer at the Alan Turing Institute, who has worked extensively on projects including Living with Machines, the Research Software Engineering Capability Roadmap and the RSE Community, and is interested in inclusive and open data science.

Lydia France is an AI engineer at Marker and an Ai in Science research fellow at the University of Oxford. After obtaining her PhD in bird flight (2019) at Oxford, she worked as an AI researcher in Paris before moving back to the UK to work in responsible AI. She is interested in advancing creative practice through data and AI, and supported by People's Palace Projects, The Art of Cultural Exchange, t0 team, and Data/Culture.

Why are they facilitating these conversations?

We are passionate about art and technology but we have seen the recent advances increasing the digital divide. Those who had experience and felt confident to onboard the AI boat have done so. But what about those who have been excluded by narratives of programming culture, or simply didn't have access to the tools? We see conversations about copyright, the future of practice, funding, and visibility in an age of algorithmic change being had in corners of the internet far away from where many creative practitioners spend their time. These conversations are motivation to bridge that divide — to create a space where artists, researchers and technologists can meet on equal ground, ask critical questions and imagine alternative possible futures together.
